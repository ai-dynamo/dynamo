\section{Baselines}
\label{sec:baselines}

% src: CR/CONTRACT.md A2.1; CR/facts/gate.json full_plan.tiers.A_headline_core,

The headline compares the learned policy with the best heuristic an operator could deploy after
tuning it on the same data. That heuristic is chosen from eleven tuned configurations, each tuned
exactly like M1 (\cref{alg:train,sec:budget}): three restarts of $\budget = 400$ evaluations, the
same objective, train cells, replicates and validation rule. Ten are the catalog policies of
\cref{tab:baselines}; the eleventh is \policy{ramjet} with a jointly tuned router queue threshold,
the baseline arm of ablation (a). Fresh validation replicates then pick $\pibase$ among the eleven
selected configurations (\cref{sec:selection}); it was tuned \policy{ramjet}. Every heuristic is
also evaluated untuned, at its shipped defaults, and round-robin is evaluated as a floor; these rows
are reported for context.

\subsection{What is tuned}
\label{sec:baseline-spaces}

% src: CR/runs/phase2/spaces/*.yaml (ranges, scales, inits, pins, sigma0, maxstd);
%      CR/facts/phase2_prep.json spaces, decisions
Each baseline searches its shipped numeric parameters over ranges set in the parameter's own
units, log-scaled where a parameter spans orders of magnitude, starting from the shipped
defaults (\cref{tab:baseline-spaces}); restarts 1, 2 and 3 use CMA-ES seeds 1, 2 and 3. Four
kinds of parameter are pinned rather than searched.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Rescalings.} A parameter that only rescales others adds a direction along which
    decisions do not change, and CMA-ES's step size drifts along such directions
    \citep{hansen2016tutorial}. Three are pinned. Multiplying \policy{ramjet}'s
    \code{affinity\_block\_tokens} by some factor while dividing \code{max\_affinity\_blocks} and
    \code{alpha} by the same factor leaves its decisions unchanged up to integer rounding; scaling
    all three \policy{llm-d-precise-prefix} weights never changes its choice; and with the throughput
    source, \policy{llm-d-optimized-baseline}'s gate compares a token difference divided by
    \code{peak\_prefill\_tokens\_per\_second} with \code{max\_ttft\_penalty\_ms}, so only their
    product matters.
  \item \textbf{Categorical choices.} CMA-ES searches continuous coordinates, so
    \policy{lmetric}'s hot-spot detector stays on, \policy{ramjet}'s affinity basis stays
    \code{relative} and the optimized baseline's TTFT source stays \code{throughput}.
  \item \textbf{Capacity.} The sticky-session map keeps its \num{65536}-session bound.
  \item \textbf{Inputs replay does not supply.} \policy{llm-d-precise-prefix}'s per-class weights
    stay empty, because replay sets no policy class.
\end{itemize}

The initial step size $\sigmazero$ follows the rule in \cref{sec:space}: it is the step size at
which one CMA-ES step changes 17.5\% of decisions on candidate tables reconstructed from the
pilot's default-router replays. Five spaces do not reach that rate even at 0.3, the cap on the
per-coordinate standard deviation, and start at 0.3: two-tier (0.083 of decisions change),
\policy{lmetric} (0.0006), \policy{ramjet} (0.133), \policy{dualmap} (0.018) and the optimized
baseline (0.034). For \policy{chwbl} and \policy{dualmap}, the hashed key depth was held at its
default for this measurement. Any change of that depth redraws the prefix-to-worker map, so the
objective is flat between block boundaries and jumps across them: 0.69 and 0.18 of decisions
change already at a step of 0.03. \Cref{tab:leaderboard-full} in the appendix gives each tuned
baseline's selected restart and checkpoint with its validation and test scores; the selected
\policy{ramjet}, for instance, came from restart 3 at generation 25, with \code{alpha} 0.883,
\code{max\_affinity\_blocks} 68 and \code{load\_unit\_tokens} 4552 (\cref{fig:api}).

\subsection{Ports, not reproductions}
\label{sec:ports}

% src: CR/CONTRACT.md A2.5 ("Baselines are evaluated as implemented on this branch. Do not reproduce
%      paper numbers."); CR/literature/LESSONS.md LR-04 (Henderson pdf p.5, VERIFIED)
By operator decision, the ports are evaluated as implemented: we built no paper-faithful variants
and did not try to reproduce published numbers. Implementations of the same baseline can differ
materially \citep{henderson2018matters}, so beating \policy{lmetric} or \policy{dualmap} here
means beating these ports in this simulator, at tuned and at shipped parameters, not the
published systems.

% src: CR/facts/pilot.json heuristic_screen_train_subset (replicates [0, 1], errors 0, ranking,
%      mean_clipped_log_ratio: dualmap -0.15048, chwbl -0.21350, round_robin -0.48060; sanity_note);
%      CR/facts/STATE.md audit(setup/ais-and-policy r0) ("chwbl, llm-d-precise-prefix, optimized-baseline,
%      ramjet and dualmap plus their parameters change routing deterministically")
The decision called for only a brief sanity check, that each port's decisions respond to its
inputs in the documented direction. A setup audit confirmed that the parameters of
\policy{chwbl}, \policy{dualmap}, \policy{ramjet} and both llm-d ports change their routing
deterministically, and that \policy{lmetric} and two-tier replay identically across runs. In the
pilot's screen of the nine untuned heuristics on 12 train
cells with replicates 0 and 1, every port ran without error, none routed against its documented
direction in aggregate, and seven beat $\piref$. \policy{dualmap} and \policy{chwbl} lost to it,
with mean clipped log-ratios of $-0.150$ and $-0.214$, which the pilot attributed to hash-pinned
placement on traffic with few hot prefixes; round-robin scored $-0.481$. These screen numbers are
preliminary (pilot, train subset).

% src: code module docs of each port; CR/literature/LESSONS.md LR-04 (LMetric pdf p.2, p.8-9;
%      DualMap pdf p.7; VERIFIED)
The known differences between the ports and the published methods are these.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \policy{lmetric}, as evaluated, omits the prefill already queued on a worker from its
    prefill factor. The paper credits that term for its advantage over a cache-hit-ratio indicator
    \citep{zhang2026lmetric}. The port also counts its hot-spot window in decisions rather than
    time. After the campaign froze its results, the port in ai-dynamo/dynamo\#15450 was fixed to
    count the worker's queued prefill in the prefill factor, so it now matches the paper's
    definition of that factor. The campaign branch keeps the port as evaluated: the
    \policy{lmetric} baseline, its tuning and every \policy{lmetric} number in this paper are from
    the port before the fix; this study did not evaluate the fixed port.
    % src: CR/CONTRACT.md A21; policy-stack commit "fix(router-plugins): count queued prefill in
    %      lmetric's P-token" (on #15450, after the campaign froze; not on the campaign branch)
  \item \policy{dualmap}'s budget is a fixed default; the paper derives it as the prefill backlog
    a GPU can clear within the TTFT SLO \citep{yuan2026dualmap}, and our SLO has no TTFT term.
    The port uses rendezvous hashing in place of the paper's consistent-hash rings, and it omits
    the paper's migration of queued requests between the two candidates, because Dynamo places
    each request once.
  \item \policy{ramjet} approximates Ramjet's per-request load units from per-worker load, reads
    Dynamo's KV-event index instead of Ramjet's own approximate prefix index, and omits Ramjet's
    lane that confines very long prompts to designated replicas.
  \item \policy{llm-d-precise-prefix}'s queue score counts running as well as waiting requests,
    where llm-d reads the engine's waiting queue.
  \item \policy{llm-d-optimized-baseline}'s prefill rate is llm-d's hardware calibration for this
    model, GPU and parallelism, while replay times prefill with \ais{}; the port also omits
    llm-d's optional exploration probability.
  \item Two-tier reads Dynamo's KV-event overlap instead of the SGLang router's approximate cache
    history, and breaks ties by worker ID instead of the router's worker-list order.
  \item \policy{chwbl} replaces the hash ring with rendezvous hashing.
\end{itemize}
% src: CR/runs/phase2/spaces/{dualmap,llmd_optimized_baseline}.yaml (budget searched; peak pinned,
%      penalty searched, "only their product matters")
Tuning absorbs two of these: \policy{dualmap}'s budget is searched directly, and a miscalibrated
prefill rate in the optimized baseline is equivalent to a different TTFT penalty, which is
searched. The missing queued-prefill term in \policy{lmetric} as evaluated is structural, and no
parameter setting adds it.
% src: FEATURES.md "Representability" (theta = -(e_log_ptok + e_log_bs) takes the argmin of
%      (active_prefill + new_prefill) x (active_requests + 1)); CR/CONTRACT.md A17.1 (M1-v2, init s3)
The learned family is not limited the same way. Feature set \code{v2} can express the paper's
product with the queued term: $\theta = -(\basis{8} + \basis{10})$, on \code{log\_ptok} and
\code{log\_bs} (\cref{tab:features-v2}), takes its argmin. The arm M1-v2, one of the candidates
for the headline learned model, uses \code{v2}, and one of its restarts starts near that point
(\cref{sec:ladder}). M1-v2 was selected as the headline learned arm, so the headline policy can
represent a variant of LMetric that the \policy{lmetric} baseline cannot. This asymmetry bounds the
headline's scope (\cref{sec:res-fairness}).

% src: WT/notes/learned-routing/REPRODUCE.md "Policy seeds" (SEEDED_TYPES = dynamo-default-cost-fn,
%      learned-choice, sticky-session; seed k + 1); tie-break code: two_tier_cost_fn.rs, lmetric.rs,
%      dualmap.rs, chwbl.rs (lowest worker ID); ramjet.rs, llm_d/mod.rs (rotate_by_worker)
Only \defaultcf{} and \policy{sticky-session} draw random numbers, for ties and softmax samples;
the harness seeds them with $\krep + 1$ on replicate $\krep$, as it seeds \lc{}. The other
policies break ties by worker ID or by a rotating counter. Every baseline therefore replays
identically on a given replicate.

\subsection{Excluded and secondary baselines}
\label{sec:excluded}

% src: WT/docs/fern/.../configuration-and-tuning.md ("Offline replay runs the worker-selection policies
%      above but not request classifiers"); CR/CONTRACT.md A2.5; LESSONS.md LR-04 action 4 and
%      LR-07 (SMetric pdf p.6, p.10, VERIFIED)
ThunderAgent's worker selection \citep{kang2026thunderagent} follows the preference of a paired
request classifier, and replay runs worker-selection policies but not classifiers, so it is not
evaluated and lies outside every claim. SMetric \citep{wang2026smetric} is not in the router catalog, and the operator
decision rules out building new implementations; its published advantage also assumes a global
KV tier, which replay does not have.

% src: CR/CONTRACT.md A16 (AIS league: M0-ais, llm-d-optimized-baseline ttft_source modeled with the AIS
%      load model, each 3 restarts at B = 400; same normalizer default@defaults without AIS; headline
%      unchanged); llm_d/optimized_baseline.rs (TtftSource::Modeled, worker without a prediction = slowest)
A later amendment allows new arms to read signals from \ais{}, the performance model that times
replay, in a separate league with its own leaderboard. Its baselines are M0 run with the router's
\ais{} prefill-load model (\code{router\_prefill\_load\_model: ais}) and the optimized baseline with
\code{ttft\_source: modeled}, which estimates each worker's TTFT from that model's prefill backlog
and counts a worker without an estimate as slowest. Both are tuned with the same budget and
normalized by the same $\piref$ without \ais{}. Because these policies read estimates from the
model that also times the simulation, the league is a secondary comparison and never enters the
headline. Running the default cost with the \ais{} load model is not neutral: it changed the default
router's own routing on 12 of 12 probe pairs, so M0-ais shows that effect explicitly. All four arms
of the league ran with three restarts at the same budget; results are in \cref{sec:res-ais}.

\paragraph{Fairness.}
% src: tab:baseline-spaces sources; CR/runs/phase2/spaces/lmetric.yaml (comment: "the hot-spot detector
%      rarely alarms because every worker holds the few hot class prefixes"); CR/CONTRACT.md A11.1, A12;
%      CR/facts/phase2_prep.json m1_inits
Equal budget does not mean equal search difficulty. The baseline spaces have 2 to 6 free
parameters against M1's 7 and M1-v2's 22. In five of them a CMA-ES step changes few decisions even at the
largest allowed step. \policy{lmetric}'s two parameters act only through its hot-spot detector,
which on these workloads rarely alarms; the space's notes attribute this to every worker holding
the few hot class prefixes, so tuned \policy{lmetric} should stay close to its defaults. The
hashed key depths of \policy{chwbl} and \policy{dualmap} make their objectives jump. The
equal-budget rule fixes what each policy may spend, not how far each can get. In the other
direction, the learned arms' restarts start from the default router, a behavior clone of the
strongest untuned heuristic in the pilot (\policy{llm-d-precise-prefix}) and a third informed point,
for M1-v2 the faithful LMetric point (\cref{sec:restarts}), while every baseline restarts from its
shipped defaults. These starting points cost no objective evaluations but carry information from
heuristic decisions, so the test also reports M1 restricted to its default start (Amendment A12), and
\cref{sec:res-fairness} shows how much of the learned margin the faithful LMetric point carries by
itself. No baseline was still improving at the end of its budget (\cref{sec:budget}).
