\section{Major caveats}
\label{app:caveats}

Building, calibrating and auditing the study surfaced a number of properties of the API, of the
replay host and of the workloads that can change which policy wins. \Cref{tab:caveats} lists the
major ones with their evidence and status: \emph{fixed} (a fix exists on the study's branch or
upstream), \emph{follow-up} (recorded for an upstream change, worked around here), or
\emph{inherent} (a property of the setting that this study can only scope). Several of them shape
the results in later sections, which refer to them by row number; \cref{sec:caveats} highlights
those.

1 & Replay's router state is perfectly fresh. &
  Replay applies KV events, prefill completions and request completions to the router
  synchronously, so policies always see exact overlap and load; a live router sees them late.
  Policies that lean on exact state could lose their advantage live. &
  Follow-up: a lag knob on a separate branch; M1-v2 stays first and ahead of \policy{ramjet} at
  10--\SI{200}{ms} on test and ahead at \SI{1000}{ms} on validation (\cref{sec:res-robustness}),
  and the live ranking agrees with replay's on six test cells (\cref{sec:live}). \\
2 & \code{expected\_output\_tokens} equals the true output length in replay. &
  The replay host fills the field from the trace's output length, a value no live router has.
  A policy reading it would see the future. &
  Inherent to replay; no learned feature reads it, and two leakage audits found no decision that
  depends on it (\cref{sec:integration}). \\
3 & Prefix hashes are unique per request when the host does not assume KV reuse. &
  With \code{router\_assume\_kv\_reuse=false} the tracking hashes are random, so prefix-keyed
  policies (\policy{chwbl}, \policy{dualmap}, \policy{lmetric}'s classes, \lc{}'s
  \code{hash\_home}) lose their keys and decode-block accounting changes; turning off
  \code{router\_track\_prefill\_tokens} zeroes the active-prefill features. &
  Inherent to the API contract, documented in the policy stack; the learned policy must run with
  the training-time host knobs (\cref{sec:integration}). \\
4 & Synthesized closed-loop session IDs reach the router. &
  For a flat trace in closed loop, replay gives every request a single-use session ID and passes
  it to the router; the same trace in open loop, or a live client, sends none. A session-keyed
  feature then behaved differently by load mode (2 against 8 workers per prefix key). &
  Follow-up: a fix with a regression test is in AISimulate pull request \#378, and Dynamo needs a pin
  bump after its release; the learned feature was re-keyed on the prompt prefix. \\
5 & One public trace is a relabeled copy of another. &
  The FAST'25 tool-agent trace matches Mooncake on all \num{23608} output lengths, maps one-to-one
  onto its hash blocks and scales its timestamps by 0.9825. It would have put one workload in two
  families and possibly two splits. & Fixed: excluded from every split (\cref{sec:workloads}). \\
6 & The default router's tie-breaks are unseeded. &
  Identical replays of one cell gave goodput 2.8934 and 2.8290 under the catalog default, so
  re-runs could not be paired. &
  Fixed on the study's branch (a \code{seed} parameter); follow-up for the policy stack. \\
7 & Open-loop agent traffic has a load cliff. &
  With Poisson play arrivals, between 0.002 and 0.0025 plays per second per worker the number of
  plays in flight grows without bound at every $\Nw$, so no stationary open-loop load reaches the
  heaviest calibration band. & Inherent: AgentX runs only in agentic lanes
  (\cref{sec:agentx}). \\
8 & The slowdown clause has a knife edge at slowdown 1, and drain-window scoring dilutes
  differences. &
  A request that ran alone has $\EtoE = \Ezero$ exactly; float noise and an off-by-one decode
  context made it fail a strict comparison in a policy-dependent way (round-robin's ratio 0.57
  instead of 0.88 on one cell). Scoring closed-loop windows through the end-of-run drain diluted
  policy differences by 23--41\%, once flipping a sign. &
  Fixed: $\Ezero$ aligned with replay's decode context plus a $10^{-6}$ relative tolerance; windows
  end at full occupancy (\cref{sec:objective}). \\
9 & Logged arrival timestamps are quantized. &
  Mooncake's arrivals sit on a \SI{3053}{ms} grid, with up to 47 requests per instant, and replay
  delivers each burst at once at any speedup. At $\Nw = 2$ the default's 85th-percentile slowdown
  was 8.32 with bursts and 1.46 with arrivals spread inside their slot. &
  Follow-up: the harness spreads arrivals per replicate (\cref{sec:replicates}). \\
10 & No AgentX play fits a 32K context. &
  Every one of the 82 plays that fits the \num{131072}-token context peaks at \num{44048} tokens or
  more, so the deployment runs at 128K with YaRN, where \ais{}'s accuracy is least validated. &
  Inherent; disclosed (\cref{sec:threats-sim}). \\
11 & The per-request temperature override is ignored by the default policy. &
  The plugin default picker reads only its own temperature; a documentation example still shows
  the override. & Intentional upstream; documentation follow-up. \\
12 & The policy file could change between hashing and use. &
  A policy file edited during a replay benchmark run could be reported under a hash that does not
  match the policy that ran. & Fixed in the policy-stack pull request
  ai-dynamo/\allowbreak{}dynamo\#15450: the benchmark hashes the file once and rejects a change
  before reporting. \\
13 & \aisim{} prices a mixed prefill pass at the batch mean. &
  Adding a short fresh prompt to a long-context chunk makes the pass cheaper than the chunk alone
  (0.73--0.82$\times$ in four probes), a simulator artifact a search could exploit. &
  Follow-up (not patched, it would change every cached result); the exploit check is negative for
  the headline policy (\cref{sec:gaming}). \\
14 & Catalog policies saw no device-tier overlap in replay on Dynamo's main branch. &
  Replay left the device-tier overlap at zero, which reduces every cache-aware port to load-only
  routing. & Fixed in the policy stack this study builds on, with a regression test. \\
15 & A request-count objective can be gamed. &
  The first tuned learned model kept one worker whose requests were all bad
  (\cref{sec:gaming}). & Remedied by a sign constraint on load coefficients; the remaining
  concentration is reported on every learned row. \\
\end{longtable}
