The 30 cells at unseen worker counts also lie on held-out segments: 14 on held-out Mooncake
windows, 8 on held-out session seeds and 8 on held-out AgentX plays. The 10 transform cells use
values outside the train ranges: prompt stretches of 2.5 (three cells), output-length multipliers

many prefix roots. Training sees only $\Nw \in \{4, 8\}$, so $\Nw = 2$, 16 and 32 are
extrapolation and $\Nw = 6$ is interpolation. Validation also has 4 cells at $\Nw = 6$, so every
configuration was selected with that worker count in view, and the 7 test cells at $\Nw = 6$ are
labeled \emph{selection-exposed} \citep{cawley2010overfitting}.

\subsection{Noise-floor measurements}

% src: CR/facts/noise.json why; CR/literature/LESSONS.md LR-02 [setup] (runs/setup/determinism/seeded/
%      determinism.json: n8_default_seed1 2.8687, seed2 2.8180; spread 1.80%)
Changing only the default router's tie-break seed moved its goodput by 1.8\% on an 8-worker setup
cell, which is why every policy runs seeded (\cref{sec:noise-floor}).
% prov: A1 (identical re-runs cannot measure noise; CRN workload replicates instead)
Over 8 replicates per cell at calibrated loads, $\piref$'s windowed goodput had a coefficient of
variation between 0.37\% and 6.5\% across the 48 train and validation cells (median 1.4\%). The
paired ratio of round-robin to $\piref$ on the same replicate had a pooled standard deviation of
0.027 on train and 0.026 on validation.
% src: CR/facts/noise_calibration.json per_cell_default_goodput_cv (48 non-noise cells: min 0.003743
%      mooncake-w4-base-n8-open-L1, max 0.06535 sessions-s1-base-n4-open-L3, median 0.01376),
%      rr_vs_default_differs_beyond_noise.{train,val}.pooled_sd (0.02679, 0.02566), protocol (8 replicates)

In the pilot's comparison of M1 with \policy{llm-d-precise-prefix}, the standard error of the mean
over 6 validation segments (0.019, against a pooled replicate standard deviation of the paired
ratio of 0.015) was, by
the pilot's own reading, dominated by differences between segments. The mean lead of $+0.030$ came
almost entirely from one Mooncake segment ($+0.102$), and M1 trailed on all three AgentX segments
($-0.012$ to $-0.025$); these numbers are \prelim.

%      0.01510, segment_se 0.01908, mean_clipped_log_ratio 0.02978), mde.note ("SE is dominated by
%      between-segment heterogeneity ... not by replicate noise"); CR/facts/gate.json
%      audit_flags_for_stage4[1] (mooncake:w1 +0.102; AgentX -0.012 to -0.025)

% src: CR/CONTRACT.md A1 ("differs beyond noise"); CR/facts/noise.json rules.differs_beyond_noise;
%      CR/facts/gate.json rule
% prov: "Amendment A1's two-clause rule", applied at the pilot gate
At the end of the pilot, a two-clause noise rule decided whether a policy differs from $\piref$: it
does if on some validation cell its mean paired ratio differs from 1 by more than three pooled
replicate standard deviations, \emph{and} the mean of its per-segment deltas exceeds two standard
errors over at least three segments. The headline test (\cref{sec:headline}) replaces this rule for
the final comparison.

% src: CR/facts/test_freeze.json (test_jsonl sha256 7b998b8e..., cell_content_sha256 60 entries, traces 39,

The test split was frozen at calibration. The freeze record holds the SHA-256 of the test cell file,
of each of the 60 cells' contents, of the 39 test traces and their 7 sources, of the engine
configuration and of the split manifest, together with the bindings build and harness version; all
of them (149 of 149) were verified before the single test pass (\cref{sec:freeze,app:repro}).
A completeness audit later found that the test cells were touched once: the \num{30060} test-cell
records in the cache are exactly the expected set, all written after the list of finalists was frozen.
The one exception predates calibration: five default-router-only validation replays of the AgentX
trace conversion ran on test play pools, at pre-calibration loads, before the test set was frozen;
they compared no policies. Every learned variant scored on validation (rung, rank, feature set,
restart) is counted and reported: 314 variants, of which 5 arms competed for the headline
(\cref{sec:res-selection}).

\subsection{Per-worker-count significance under Holm's correction}

% src: own arithmetic (2026-10-03), assuming each per-N test is the same exact one-sided Wilcoxon test:
%      Holm first threshold 0.05/6 = 0.00833; min p with 6 segments 1/64 = 0.0156 > 0.00833; with 7
%      segments 1/128 = 0.0078 (N = 4); with 8 segments 1/256 = 0.0039 (N = 8); after rejecting both,
%      the next threshold 0.05/4 = 0.0125 < 0.0156. Segments per N from tab:test-axes.
Assuming each per-count test is the same exact test as the headline, Holm's correction across the
six worker counts leaves little room. Its first threshold is $0.05/6 \approx 0.0083$, below the
smallest $p$ attainable with 6 segments ($1/64 \approx 0.016$). Each unseen worker count, $\Nw = 2$,
6, 16 and 32, has 6 test segments, so none of them can produce a significant per-count result,
whatever the data; only $\Nw = 4$ (7 segments, all favoring one policy) and $\Nw = 8$ (8 segments),
both train worker counts, can. This is our own arithmetic, not part of the pre-registration.

\subsection{Constants of the alternative SLO definitions}

% src: CR/facts/sla_transfer_spec.json (records 160, replicates 0-7; families.mooncake.b_ttft_ms 146.74)
% prov: A14
The constants of the four alternative definitions of a good request (\cref{sec:robustness}) were
computed per family from 160 train records of $\piref$ at L2 and written down before any test record
was scored. For Mooncake, for example, the TTFT bound's intercept is $\piref$'s median TTFT there,
\SI{146.7}{ms}.

\subsection{Validation of the lag patch}

% src: CR/facts/lag_patch.json (summary, semantics, worktree, build,
%      proof_lag0_identical: 8 train cells x 4 policies x k0,1 = 64 pairs, 404,704 rows;
%      proof_lag_changes_what_policies_see.goodput_level: 50 ms changed 63/64, ratio 0.9719-1.0231;
%      200 ms changed 61/64, ratio 0.9559-1.0197; routing changed 64/64 at both; caveat;
%      remote-node parity 192/192, 1,214,112 rows; test_stage_usage)
% prov: the separate-build sentence moved here from sec:robustness
The lag patch lives in its own branch and build, so the tuning build and its result cache stay
untouched (\cref{app:repro}). On 8 train cells, 4 policies and 2 replicates (64 runs), $\lag = 0$
on the lag build reproduced the tuning build in every non-environment record field and in all \num{404704} per-request rows. At
$\lag = 50$ and \SI{200}{ms} routing changed in all 64 runs, and goodput relative to $\lag = 0$
ranged from 0.972 to 1.023 and from 0.956 to 1.020; these single-replicate ratios show that the knob
is live, not how lag affects the ranking. The robustness runs repeat the lag-0 identity check
(\cref{app:results}).

\subsection{Herding guards}

% src: CR/literature/LESSONS.md LR-14 action 2, LR-15 action 3; CR/facts/gate.json
%      full_plan.tiers.C_conditional ("robustness extras (speedup 0.8/1.2, tau > 0, hash_home)");
%      CR/facts/lag_patch.json lessons.deferred_to_test_stage
A deterministic argmax over stale state can herd requests onto one worker. Two variants guard
against it: \lc{} at $\temp > 0$, which samples instead of taking the argmax (at a fixed
temperature, the probability left to non-best workers grows with $\Nw$), and a variant restricted
to the request's hash-home workers (feature \code{hash\_home} of \code{v2}). Both were registered
before M1-v2 won, so they ran as variants of M1 (\code{v1}), at two temperatures, on the lag build.
\Cref{app:results} reports them.

\subsection{Guard metrics}

% src: WT/benchmarks/learned_routing/learned_routing/goodput.py guard_metrics; window_guards.py
%      (added by the phase-2 midpoint fix: window-basis share, per-worker good minimum, long-prompt good,
%      NMI by prompt-length quartile); CR/literature/LESSONS.md LR-13
% prov: "the midpoint fix" (phase-2 midpoint)
Every evaluation record carries guard metrics that the audits read: the largest worker's share of
requests against the cap $\min(2/\Nw,\ 1/\Nw + 0.25)$, the largest worker's prefill tokens over the
mean, the share of multi-turn sessions split across workers, the share of requests slower than three
times the slowdown bound, and the largest worker's share within each prompt-length bucket, which
exposes length segregation. Black-box search is known to exploit gaps in its objective
\citep{lehman2020creativity}; an evolution strategy tuning a router on 95th-percentile TTFT alone
sent all traffic to one replica \citep{toniolo2026gorgo}; and binary goodput invites sacrificing
requests that would miss their SLO anyway \citep{wang2024slo}. The gaming audit showed that the
original guards, computed over all rows, could not see a worker whose in-window requests were all
bad, so the mid-tuning fix added window-basis guards: the share cap over exactly the scored
requests, each worker's good fraction, the good fraction of long prompts against $\piref$, and the
normalized mutual information between worker and prompt-length quartile (\cref{sec:gaming}).

Each stage of the study ends at a checkpoint that independent auditors, each through one named lens,
review before the next stage starts (\cref{sec:audits} summarizes the outcome). A static audit reads
code and artifacts; a dynamic audit also re-runs part of the pipeline in a private result root and
recomputes results with its own code, without importing the study's harness. Findings are graded
blocker, major or minor; a fixer answers each major finding, either fixing it or rebutting it with
evidence, and the lens is audited again.

\Cref{tab:audits} lists every audit round. Through the pilot's review there were 17 audit reports
and 6 fix reports. All 10 major findings were judged
valid and none was rebutted. Nine were fixed and the fix verified by a later round; the tenth, a
second public trace that turned out to be a relabeled copy of the Mooncake trace, was resolved by
excluding that family. The pilot's review was itself checked the same way: an independent judge
recomputed every pilot number from raw records with its own scripts, reproduced each to four
decimals, and approved continuing to the main tuning. Midway through tuning, three dynamic audits
looked for overfitting, objective gaming and simulator exploitation; their five major findings were
all fixed. After the single test pass, a completeness critic and three adversarial refuters reviewed
the headline before any report was written; their two major findings were fixed by adding the
missing secondary metrics and by narrowing the headline's wording to the scope the evidence
supports.
